# LEGAL AND ETHICAL CHECKLIST (PHILIPPINES)

This checklist is a starting brief for KIN's agents, not legal advice. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer yet. Have a Philippine lawyer review it before relying on it for published episodes. The agents flag risks. Justine decides, with a lawyer for anything high risk.

Status: NOT YET REVIEWED BY A LAWYER

## 1. Libel and cyberlibel
Laws: Revised Penal Code (Articles 353 to 362), Cybercrime Prevention Act (RA 10175).
- Online publication of defamatory content can be prosecuted as cyberlibel, which carries heavier penalties than print libel.
- Anyone not convicted is "accused", "suspect", or "respondent". Never "the killer" or "the culprit".
- If a person was acquitted, the case was dismissed, or charges were dropped, say so clearly.
- Attribute every allegation: "police said", "the complaint alleged", "according to court records".
- Do not imply guilt through editing, music, or visuals.
- Opinion must be clearly labeled as opinion and based on stated facts.

## 2. Privacy
Law: Data Privacy Act (RA 10173).
- Do not show addresses, phone numbers, ID numbers, or license plates of private individuals.
- Do not name or show relatives who are not part of the story.
- Blur faces of private individuals in footage unless they are central to the story and the information is already public.
- Public officials acting in their official role have less privacy protection than private citizens.

## 3. Minors and protected victims
Laws include: RA 7610 (child abuse), RA 9262 (violence against women and children), RA 9344 (juvenile justice), RA 9208 as amended (trafficking).
- Never reveal the name, face, school, address, or other identifying details of a child victim or a child in conflict with the law.
- Never identify victims in cases where the law protects their identity, including sexual violence and VAWC cases.
- When in doubt, anonymize: use "the victim" and change identifying details, and say on screen that details were changed.

## 4. Pending cases
- Avoid content that prejudges an ongoing trial or investigation.
- State the case status and the date you checked it.
- Re-check status before publishing. Cases move.

## 5. Copyright and footage
- Use only footage and images that are licensed, public domain, KIN-owned, or clearly within fair use after review.
- Credit sources on screen or in the description.
- Label AI-generated shots of real people or real events as "reconstruction".

## 6. Platform policy (YouTube)
- Graphic violence, real crime scene imagery, and detailed descriptions of harm can trigger age restriction or limited ads. Check YouTube's current policies before publishing, because they change.
- Avoid sensational thumbnails showing real victims or real bodies.

## 7. Ethics (KIN house rules)
- Respect victims and families. Tell their story, not just the crime.
- No victim-blaming language.
- No graphic detail that does not serve the story.
- If a family has asked publicly for privacy, consider whether the episode should be made at all.

## Risk levels for flags
- HIGH: possible libel, identifying a protected victim or minor, prejudging a pending case. Stop. Justine and a lawyer review.
- MEDIUM: unattributed allegation, private details, unlicensed footage. Fix before editors start.
- LOW: tone, sensitivity, ad suitability. Fix during the edit.
