# SOURCE STANDARDS

Every factual claim in a KIN documentary needs a source from tier 1 to 3. Tiers 4 and 5 help you find leads but cannot carry a claim alone, except folklore, which may rest on tier 4.

## Source tiers (strongest first)
| Tier | Source type | Examples | How to use |
| 1 | Court decisions and official records | Supreme Court decisions, case records, PSA data, government reports, Senate and House records | Strongest. Quote case numbers and dates. |
| 2 | Official statements | PNP, NBI, DOJ, LGU press releases and briefings | One side's account. Say who said it. |
| 3 | Established news with named reporters | National outlets, wire services, reputable regional papers | Prefer 2 or more outlets that agree. Note the date: early reports are often wrong. |
| 4 | Books and academic work | Published histories, folklore studies, theses, journal articles | Strong for history and folklore. Check the author's credentials. |
| 5 | Leads only | Wikipedia, forums, Reddit, Facebook posts, TikTok, other YouTube videos, blogs | Never a source. Follow the link to the original. |

## Rules
- Open every page you cite. A search snippet is not a source.
- Record for every source: title, outlet or author, date published, link, date accessed, tier.
- Contested claims need sources from both sides, and the script must show both.
- Numbers (deaths, dates, amounts) need tier 1 to 3. If sources disagree, say so and give the range.
- Quotes need the exact wording and the original speaker. Otherwise paraphrase.
- Folklore and belief are labeled as belief ("locals say", "the story goes"), never stated as fact.
- If a claim cannot be sourced, it is cut or marked [VERIFY] until it is.

## Citation format
[S#] Title. Outlet or author. Date published. Link. Accessed YYYY-MM-DD. Tier #.
