# KIN SERIES BIBLE

## The promise
KIN tells the full story behind the mysteries, rises, falls, and businesses people only know from headlines. Every video answers one question the viewer already has, and ends with something they did not know.

## Who we make it for
- English speaking viewers, 18 to 35, worldwide.
- They already watch story channels like SunnyV2 and similar creators.
- They want the whole story in one place, told clearly, with receipts.
- They hate filler, fake suspense, and clickbait that does not pay off.

## Pillars
| Pillar | What it covers | The question it answers |
| Mysteries | Unsolved cases, strange events, internet mysteries, disappearances of things, not just people | What really happened? |
| Rise and fall | Creators, celebrities, brands and trends that blew up then collapsed | How did they get so big, and what went wrong? |
| Business stories | How companies were built, broke, or changed an industry | How did this actually work, and why did it win or lose? |

Rotate the pillars. Never post two of the same pillar in a row unless Justine decides otherwise.

## Episode format (about 20 minutes)
| Part | Time | Job |
| Cold open | 0:00 to 0:45 | The most shocking or intriguing moment. No intro, no logo, no "in this video". |
| Setup | 0:45 to 3:00 | Who or what this is, why it matters, and the question we will answer. |
| Chapter 1: The beginning | 3:00 to 7:00 | Origins and the rise. Make the viewer care. |
| Chapter 2: The peak | 7:00 to 11:00 | At their biggest. Plant the cracks. |
| Chapter 3: The turn | 11:00 to 16:00 | What went wrong, or the mystery deepens. Strongest evidence here. |
| Chapter 4: The fallout | 16:00 to 19:00 | Consequences, what happened next, where things stand now. |
| Ending | 19:00 to 20:00 | The answer, the lesson, a final line that lands. Point to the next video. |
Re-hook every 60 to 90 seconds: a reveal, a twist, a contrast, or a new character.

## Visual language
- Motion graphics and 3D made in-house are the signature. Use them for timelines, maps, numbers, org charts, money flows, and recreating moments we have no footage of.
- Internet B-roll and clips are evidence: show the actual post, video, headline, or document being discussed.
- NO AI-generated visuals, ever.
- Consistent look: one font system, one color palette, recurring graphic elements (to be set by Justine in the KIN design kit).

## Footage rules
- Use clips only to comment on, criticize, or explain them. Never let a clip play just as entertainment.
- Keep clips as short as the point needs, with narration or graphics over or around them.
- Record the source link of every clip in the script. Credit sources in the description.
- If a creator or company issues a takedown or Content ID claim, flag it to Justine. Do not argue it without her decision.

## Fairness rules
- We tell the story, we do not pile on. Show the subject's side and any apology, response, or correction.
- Allegations are attributed, never stated as fact.
- No mocking appearance, mental health, family, or private life that is not part of the story.
- If new facts change the story after upload, pin a correction.

## Packaging
- Titles: a name plus a tension. Examples of the pattern: "The Rise and Fall of [Name]", "What Really Happened to [Name]", "How [Company] Lost Everything", "The Mystery of [Event]".
- Thumbnails: one face or object, max 4 words, one strong emotion, high contrast. No fake screenshots or invented quotes.
- The title and thumbnail must be true to what the video shows.

## Shorts
- 3 to 5 Shorts per long video, 30 to 60 seconds each.
- Each works alone: its own hook in the first 2 seconds, one story beat, and a reason to watch the full video.

## KIN Creatives showcase
- Description line on every long video: "Edited and animated by KIN Creatives" with the KIN Creatives link.
- The craft is the advertisement. No hard selling inside the video.
